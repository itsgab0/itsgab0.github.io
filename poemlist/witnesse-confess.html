    Witneſse/Confeſs

    Gabe Foad — VI.2025

    Turbid rain fell opaque as night, plunging the world into a deep darkness. Men dragged their feet behind them as they stumbled down and across the cobbled paths. Each step inevitably splashed into a puddle of murky, brown water. People were used to the downpours by now; they were familiar with the skies that preceeded the torrents, recognised the signs that it was letting up, and knew that today was no different.

    Within the heavy rainfall, most shops and stalls were closed up, and the slackers were hurriedly securing anything that wasn't tied down. The footfall sprayed muck onto the bases of legs and buildings alike, coating the whole town in a fetid odour. On these dreary days, people's eyelines were down to the ground, as one's footing was more important than eye contact. With so many bodies pushing around each other, it was impressive that no-one perceived anyone else.

    In contrast to the rest of the crowd, a hunched over old man trudged slowly through the mud. Passersby may well have assumed his cloak was made from a potato sack, the burlap material fitting incredibly loosely over his shoulders. Beneath him, the plain leather sandals slapped ungracefully on the ground as the increasingly liquid mud passed between the straps, over his toes, and back onto the ground. His cane pressed deep into the sludge with every step, yet he expressed no difficulty retrieving it and pushing forwards; if anything, it was as if for him the mud wasn't there at all. But no-one noticed, for no-one was looking.

    The inclement weather worsened and the crowds of people on the streets thinned, but still the old man proceeded towards some unknown destination. Without even thinking about it, people were inclined to avoid him. If asked about it, they would not remember even seeing the man; and in the moments immediately succeeding the encounter, they would say something like:

    "I think I saw a signpost out of the corner of my eye."

    But from under a poorly-constructed wooden canopy across the street, a young man was watching. His tunic was far from waterproof, but he needed to prove a point. None of his friends had believed the stories of the Lonely Wanderer, dismissing it as folklore, or religious scaremongering. But Godfrey knew better. He'd followed him for days and observed the way people moved around him like he wasn't there, yet some voice in the back of their minds subtly influenced them to move out of his way. And he never once lifted his head, nor moved his hands in front to bid people respect his personal space.

    Finally, Godfrey worked up the courage to confront the man. For a moment, doubt flickered across his sun-kissed face; what if he approached him and forgot why he had done so? Would he become like all the others who didn't even remember that they'd seen him? The rain immediately started pouring down on him as he stepped out from under his wooden sanctuary. Water fell through his hair and tried to cover his eyes, but he wiped the moisture away before it could obscure his vision. However, his mission was clear: he mustn't lose sight of the man.

    Under his left hand, the pommel of a steel blade was cold but reassuring. It reminded Godfrey that he was still alive, and still awake. Most people moved out of his way, but he did bump shoulders with one or two hapless townsfolk. Clearly, he didn't exhibit the same pseudo-magical powers as the Wanderer. Not once did his eyes leave the target of his investigation. Just a few steps away and suddenly he felt a deep cold in his chest. The man was trying to push him away, consciously or not. But Godfrey would not acquiesce that easily.

    He breathed out and squeezed on the base of his sword harder than ever before, and he pushed through the chill. The rain seemed to stop as he became focused solely on approaching the mysterious wanderer. His head began to pound with noise but he continued onwards; desperate to find out the truth. What was this man hiding?

    Feet aligned and both men came to a stop. Godfrey was surprised — this was the first time he'd seen the man actually stand still the whole time he'd been watching him. The cloaked figure did not raise his head, nor turn his body — in fact, to Godfrey he didn't appear to have even noticed he was standing beside him. And yet, he must've; otherwise, he wouldn't have stopped at all. Suddenly, the noise in Godfrey's head gave way to silence.

    He heard a soft mutter. The man was speaking to him. Or at him. He leaned in to hear what the man was saying but he still couldn't make it out. He certainly spoke the same language as Godfrey, and yet he couldn't make out a single word within the old one's mumbling. And then he heard.

    "What did you just say?" Godfrey asked him aggressively.

    His hand drew his sword and pointed it towards the old man's chest, although they were stood right next to each other. The man continued to mumble incohesively to the outside observer, and Godfrey leaned in once again. And then he heard once again.

    Godfrey took his sword and pointed it backwards towards his own chest, and in one swift motion, plunged it deep into his stomach. A thick, warm crimson came pouring out of the wound as he fell to his knees. Coughing and spluttering, he understood in that moment what it meant to be alive. Finally, he keeled over completely. The man stood above him, unwavering and unnoticing; possibly still muttering under his breath.


    The streets were empty now, but for the body of a young man who lay dead on the ground. A sword was embedded deep into his torso, but no-one saw what had happened. In the morning after the storm, a local woman would notice and alert the local gravediggers who would prepare a small pit. His family would not be notified, and his grave would remain unmarked until the tombstone wore away into dust.
